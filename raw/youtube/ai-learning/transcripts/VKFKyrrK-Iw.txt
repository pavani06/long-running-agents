This is a trace.
A record of the action an agent takes from input to final output.
Most teams building agents today have traces.
What they don't have is an answer to the question that actually matters.
How should I improve my agent?
So how should you?
Over the years we've seen the best organizations figure out how to use their traces to build
an agent development lifecycle, returns experimentation, and a repeatable system for shipping, logistics,
learning and improving over time.
That lifecycle has four parts.
You start by building the agent, of course.
Then testing your agent using data sets that cover a wide
range of examples and evals that focus on the metrics you
think determine success.
You can be as thorough as you want in your testing.
The reality is you can't predict every message from your
customers or every action from your agent.
That's why after deploying your agent, you need to monitor
through tracing and online evals.
This informs changes that need to be made,
which you can patch in, test for regressions,
deploy and monitor all over again.
LangChain has built LangSmith and the rest of its ecosystem
to facilitate teams at every point
along this development life cycle.
Up until now though, we still relied on engineers
to connect the dots between each stage.
We've seen the bar for agent quality
get higher every week,
when agent's doing more for users.
But agent engineering remains manual, slow, full of blind spots.
That's why we built Engine, an agent built
to improve your agent for you by following the agent development
lifecycle.
Engine investigates your traces, paying close attention
to any explicit errors, eval failures, negative user
feedback, or new behaviors the agent doesn't handle well yet.
When it spots a problem, it looks for the same pattern
across your project and clusters everything into an issue.
But this goes beyond triage.
If you connect your agent's repo,
engine will draft a PR with a targeted change,
then propose examples and an eval to add to your test suite
to ensure future regressions don't happen.
Once you reviewed the change and merged,
engine continues to monitor the issue in production
to verify it's actually been resolved.
Teams like Vanta, Campfire, and Cogent
have already surfaced thousands of agent issues using Engine.
They're catching aggressions earlier,
shipping fixes faster,
and spending less time on Treeout.
We're working towards a future
where agents improve themselves,
where every trace makes your system smarter,
every issue makes them more resilient,
and teams spend their time on what's next.
